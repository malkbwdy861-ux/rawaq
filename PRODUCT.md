# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary audience is Arabic-speaking residential property owners in Jeddah and Makkah who need durable outdoor shading, privacy, enclosure, or related construction work and want confidence before contacting a provider. Commercial buyers, contractors, facilities teams, and procurement decision-makers are supported as a secondary audience. Visitors need to understand suitable services, solutions, materials, pricing factors, and real project outcomes, then contact the company quickly by phone or WhatsApp.

The operational audience is the authenticated administrator who manages content, media, SEO, redirects, fixed pages, and settings through the custom CMS. The dashboard is a product-register surface inside this brand-register project and prioritizes efficient, predictable Draft, Preview, Publish, and Archive workflows.

## Product Purpose

Create a professional Arabic-only marketing and lead-generation website for a single Saudi company serving Jeddah and Makkah. The public site explains outdoor shading, enclosure, installation, maintenance, and related construction services; establishes trust through real projects and factual material information; answers commercial questions through useful guides and FAQs; supports organic discovery; and converts visitors through phone and structured WhatsApp contact.

The same Next.js application includes a custom CMS that lets an administrator publish and grow Services, Solutions, Materials, Projects, Guides, FAQs, and fixed pages without code changes. Published content remains stable while Draft changes are prepared and previewed. The product is not a marketplace, CRM, customer portal, quotation system, page builder, or general-purpose CMS.

Success means qualified visitors can determine whether the company fits their need, find credible evidence of execution, and choose an appropriate contact path without confusion. Administrators can keep public content accurate and current without risking Draft leakage or breaking established URL and SEO behavior.

## Brand Personality

Architectural, grounded, exact, durable, locally rooted, and quietly premium. The company should feel technically competent and careful rather than flashy. Premium perception comes from measured detail, honest materials, real project evidence, and disciplined execution, not luxury affectation. The lasting impression is: built correctly, lasts.

Named anchors are Al-Balad wooden roshan screens, Red Sea limestone, and precise architectural drawings. These references should inform proportion, shade, texture, rhythm, and attention to construction detail without becoming decorative pastiche.

The final logo asset is pending. The design system may reserve flexible Arabic wordmark and compact-mark zones, but it must not invent a company name, monogram, seal, or logo artwork.

## Anti-references

- Generic contractor templates with stock roofs, yellow-and-black construction clichés, crowded service tiles, and bargain-led messaging.
- Generic SaaS presentation, identical card grids, gradient text, decorative glass effects, hero-metric templates, and interchangeable AI-generated layouts.
- Luxury showroom or editorial affectation that hides practical capability behind precious styling.
- Loud promotional styling, excessive badges, urgency banners, decorative icons, and competing calls to action.
- Fabricated prices, project facts, technical claims, service areas, branches, or proof points.

## Design Principles

1. Show real proof before making claims. Project photography, factual case-study details, supported materials, and clear process information establish trust more effectively than slogans.
2. Make contact obvious without becoming aggressive. Phone and WhatsApp remain easy to find, but each page preserves one clear conversion hierarchy rather than repeating competing calls to action.
3. Guide decisions through relationships. Services, solutions, materials, projects, guides, and FAQs should connect contextually so visitors can move from a need to evidence and then to contact.
4. Let each surface serve its job. The public site may persuade with stronger composition and imagery; the dashboard should operate with calm density, familiar controls, explicit status, and consistent feedback.
5. Design Arabic and RTL behavior at the foundation. Reading order, typography, directional cues, mixed numerals, forms, tables, and media composition must work naturally in Arabic rather than being mirrored after implementation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA across the public site and dashboard. Use semantic structure, complete keyboard access, visible focus treatment, sufficient text and component contrast, touch targets of at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels where applicable, reduced-motion alternatives, explicit form labels and errors, status announcements for dynamic changes, and meaningful image alternative text. Arabic readability, text enlargement to 200%, mixed-direction content, and practical screen-reader navigation are design requirements rather than final-stage cleanup.
